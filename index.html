Email scam protection

Keep scam emails away from someone you look out for.

Quiet Protect checks every new email that arrives for an elderly parent, or anyone vulnerable to scams. Anything it thinks is unsafe is held back, and you decide what happens to it.

Quiet Protect is running in a small family pilot and is not yet open to new families.

Their inbox stays calm

Quiet reads each new email as it arrives and looks for scam tricks, like pretending to be a bank or government department, pressuring them to act now, and asking for money, passwords, or bank details. It lets safe emails through as usual, while holding back any it thinks is unsafe.

You decide what happens to anything held back

When Quiet holds an email back, you get a short email alert. Your dashboard shows who sent it, when it arrived, and why Quiet held it back, in plain English. You can release it to their inbox, mark the sender as trusted, or block them.

Their privacy comes first

You never see the email’s subject line, the message or any attachment. But you do see which senders they trust, block, or hear from often, and how much marketing they receive.

They agree to Quiet Protect themselves before it starts, and they can stop it at any time.

Honest about its limits

No email check catches everything. When Quiet isn’t sure about an email, it holds it back for you to review instead of guessing. How it works explains what Quiet looks for and what it can’t yet do.

Where their email goes

An AI model running in Amazon Web Services’ London region checks each new email. Amazon states that neither it nor the model’s maker uses the content it checks to train their models. We keep emails for a set period, then delete them. The privacy notice has the full details.

Which email accounts it works with

Quiet Protect works with Microsoft email accounts today, including Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live and Microsoft 365. Gmail and Yahoo need Google’s and Yahoo’s approval first, and we’re working on both.

Who we are

Aegient Ltd, a small UK company, makes Quiet Protect. It began with a suspicious email sent to someone in our own family.
