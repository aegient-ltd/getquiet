About

Who built Quiet Protect, why, and where it is now.

Who built this

I’m Chris Falck. I’ve spent my career building and integrating systems at scale. Quiet is the first time I’ve aimed that at a problem I’ve been living with personally.

The email scam problem is one I’ve watched affect people I love, and it felt worth solving properly.

Where Quiet Protect came from

Quiet Protect started with a question about an email.

My partner’s father is in his eighties. He’s independent, capable, and sharp enough to forward a suspicious email with a note saying, “This looks a bit weird, doesn’t it?” He was right. The email was a scam. Convincing logo, plausible language, the right sense of official urgency. He’d spotted something was off, but couldn’t quite be sure.

“I’ve just received this, it looks a bit weird.”

Knowing something felt wrong but not being certain. Having to forward an email to a family member just to check. That is exactly what Quiet Protect is designed to prevent. Not by taking away independence or making someone feel managed, but by removing the doubt before it arrives.

Scams targeting older people are getting better; scams pretending to be government bodies, banks and trusted services are increasingly convincing. Spam filters are good at blocking bulk junk. Carefully written fraud, aimed at one person, is harder to catch. We couldn’t find a product that let a family member help in this way while keeping the inbox private, so we built one.

Where it is now

Quiet Protect has been running in a small family pilot since June 2026. It is not yet open to new families. Before it opens to everyone, families need to sign up and pay on their own, and Google and Yahoo need to approve us for their email accounts.
